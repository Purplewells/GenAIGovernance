# DPIA Completion Criteria

## 1. Purpose

This standard defines when a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) for an AI use case is complete enough to support a governance decision. It does not replace DPO advice or legal review.

## 2. Required Statuses

- **Draft:** being developed; not evidence of completion and not sufficient for deployment.
- **Under DPO Review:** submitted for review; conditions and questions may remain.
- **Conditions Outstanding:** DPO review completed but specified actions remain before the relevant gate.
- **Approved:** DPO has recorded advice that the processing design and residual privacy risk are acceptable or managed, subject to stated conditions.
- **Superseded:** replaced by a later version after material change.
- **Closed:** processing ended and closure evidence is recorded.

A DPIA marked Draft or Under DPO Review must not be described elsewhere as complete.

## 3. Completion Criteria

A DPIA is complete for a stated version and scope only when it contains:

- Processing purpose, necessity, proportionality, lawful basis, and special-category condition where applicable.
- Complete data-flow map covering inputs, outputs, prompts, logs, training or improvement use, processors, subprocessors, support access, transfers, and locations.
- Data categories, data subjects, retention, deletion, accuracy, minimisation, access, security, and legal-hold requirements.
- Automated decision-making, profiling, transparency, rights, objection, correction, erasure, and recourse analysis.
- Risk assessment, mitigations, residual privacy risk, uncertainty, and conditions.
- Data-processing agreements, transfer safeguards, vendor terms, and subprocessor arrangements where applicable.
- Evidence that required controls are designed and assigned to owners.
- Written DPO advice, approval or conditions, date, scope, version, review date, and escalation decision.

## 4. Reopening Triggers

Reopen the DPIA after material change, new data source, new population, new vendor or subprocessor, new training use, transfer change, incident, complaint, rights request pattern, material drift, or relevant legal or regulatory change.
