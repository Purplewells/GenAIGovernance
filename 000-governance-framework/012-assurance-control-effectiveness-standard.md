# Assurance and Control Effectiveness Standard

## 1. Purpose

This standard defines what evidence is required before an AI control can be described as effective.

## 2. Effectiveness States

- **Not Assessed:** no sufficient review.
- **Designed:** control is specified but not verified in operation.
- **Implemented:** control is present and implementation is evidenced.
- **Partially Effective:** control operates with material limitations or exceptions.
- **Effective:** design, implementation, operating effectiveness, and proportionate independent review are evidenced for a defined period and scope.
- **Ineffective:** control fails its pass criteria or does not address the risk.
- **Expired:** evidence is outside its validity period or a review trigger occurred.

## 3. Minimum Evidence

A material control requires:

- Design review against the risk or obligation.
- Implementation verification before pilot or deployment.
- Operating-effectiveness testing using an appropriate population or sample.
- Evidence of reviewer competence and independence proportionate to risk.
- Test date, scope, sample, pass criteria, result, limitations, validity period, owner, and closure authority.

A policy, certificate, questionnaire, or vendor assertion alone cannot support an Effective rating.

## 4. Refresh and Retest

- Re-test before deployment, after material change, after a relevant incident, and at least annually for material controls.
- Re-test within six months of an incident affecting the control.
- Independent review is required for Tier 3 controls before deployment and at least every two years while operating, or sooner if risk changes.
- Evidence must state the period for which the conclusion is valid.

## 5. Human Oversight Testing

Where human oversight is material, test training completion, competence assessment, observed review of sample outputs, review time, reasoning, override capability, decision recording, escalation, and workload. Record exceptions and corrective action.

## 6. Findings and Closure

Failed or incomplete tests create a finding linked to the risk register. Closure requires remediation evidence, retest, reviewer conclusion, residual-risk decision, owner, date, and status. The control owner cannot be the sole closure authority for a material finding.
